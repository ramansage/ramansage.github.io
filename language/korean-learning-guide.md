# Korean Language Learning Guide
### A Practical, Detailed Resource for Learners

**Goal of this guide:** Give you a solid foundation in Hangul, pronunciation, essential grammar (with examples and English), high-frequency vocabulary, and learning habits so you can read, form sentences, and keep expanding.

**How to use this guide**
1. Learn Hangul first (Section 1–2) until you can sound out words.
2. Study particles and basic sentence order (Section 3–4) — Korean grammar hangs on these.
3. Learn verb/adjective endings and speech levels (Section 5–7).
4. Drill high-frequency word lists (Section 8+) daily in small batches.
5. Revisit grammar with your own example sentences; speak them out loud.

**Notation used here**
| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **한글** | Hangul (Korean writing) |
| *romanization* | Approximate pronunciation (Revised Romanization) |
| (English) | Translation / meaning |
| ~다 | Dictionary form of verbs/adjectives (e.g. 가다 = to go) |
| A / B | Alternatives or polite vs casual where noted |

> Romanization is only a bridge. Always prioritize reading Hangul.

# Part I — Writing System & Sounds

## 1. Hangul Overview

Korean uses **한글 (Hangul)**. Each syllable is written in a block combining:

- **Initial consonant** (초성)
- **Vowel** (중성)
- Optional **final consonant(s)** (받침, batchim)

Examples:
| Hangul | Breakdown | Meaning |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| 가 | ㄱ + ㅏ | *ga* — (syllable block) |
| 한 | ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ | *han* |
| 글 | ㄱ + ㅡ + ㄹ | *geul* |
| 한국 | 한 + 국 | *Hanguk* — Korea |
| 사랑 | 사 + 랑 | *sarang* — love |

### 2.5 Batchim (Final Consonants) — Essentials

Only **seven** final sounds are pronounced in isolation:
**ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, ㅇ**

| Written finals | Pronounced as | Example |
|----------------|---------------|---------|
| ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ | ㄱ [*k*] | 부엌 → *bueok* (kitchen) |
| ㄴ | ㄴ [*n*] | 산 → *san* (mountain) |
| ㄷ, ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅌ, ㅎ | ㄷ [*t*] | 옷 → *ot* (clothes) |
| ㄹ | ㄹ [*l*] | 말 → *mal* (horse/speech) |
| ㅁ | ㅁ [*m*] | 밤 → *bam* (night/chestnut) |
| ㅂ, ㅍ | ㅂ [*p*] | 입 → *ip* (mouth) |
| ㅇ | ㅇ [*ng*] | 강 → *gang* (river) |

**Linking (연음):** When a syllable ends in a consonant and the next starts with ㅇ (silent), the final moves over.

- 한국어 → *han-gu-geo* (not *han-guk-eo*)
- 있어요 → *i-sseo-yo*
- 읽어요 → *il-geo-yo* (to read — polite)

# Part II — Core Grammar

## 3. Sentence Structure

Korean is typically **SOV** (Subject–Object–Verb).

| English (SVO) | Korean (SOV) |
|---------------|--------------|
| I eat rice. | 저는 밥을 먹어요. |
| | *jeo-neun bab-eul meogeoyo.* |
| | I (topic) rice (object) eat. |

**Word order flexibility:** Topics/subjects can be dropped if clear from context. The **verb/adjective always comes at the end**.

**Basic patterns**
| Pattern | Example | English |
|---------|---------|---------|
| N + 이에요/예요 | 학생이에요. | (I/he/she) am/is a student. |
| N + 이/가 + V | 비가 와요. | Rain comes. (It's raining.) |
| N + 을/를 + V | 책을 읽어요. | (I) read a book. |
| N + 에 + V | 학교에 가요. | (I) go to school. |
| N + 에서 + V | 카페에서 만나요. | (We) meet at a café. |

---

## 4. Particles (조사) — The Backbone of Korean

Particles attach to nouns and show their role. Learn these early.

### 4.1 Topic: 은 / 는

Marks the **topic** (what you're talking about), often with contrast.

| Form | When | Example | English |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| 은 | After consonant | 저는 학생이에요. | As for me, (I) am a student. |
| 는 | After vowel | 오늘은 바빠요. | Today (topic), (I) am busy. |

**Contrast:**
- 커피는 좋아하지만 차는 싫어해요.  
  *I like coffee, but (as for) tea I dislike it.*

### 4.2 Subject: 이 / 가

Marks the **grammatical subject**, often new info or focus.

| Form | When | Example | English |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| 이 | After consonant | 누가 왔어요? | Who came? |
| 가 | After vowel | 비가 와요. | Rain is falling. |

**은/는 vs 이/가 (learner rule of thumb)**
- **은/는**: known topic, general statements, contrast  
- **이/가**: who/what did something, existence, new focus  

- 저는 학생이에요. (Introducing myself as topic)  
- 제 친구가 왔어요. (My friend is the one who came — subject focus)

### 4.3 Object: 을 / 를

| Form | When | Example | English |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| 을 | After consonant | 밥을 먹어요. | (I) eat rice/a meal. |
| 를 | After vowel | 영화를 봐요. | (I) watch a movie. |

### 4.4 Location / Time / Direction

| Particle | Meaning | Example | English |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| 에 | to / at / in (static or destination); time | 학교에 가요. / 3시에 만나요. | Go to school. / Meet at 3. |
| 에서 | at/in (place of action); from | 집에서 일해요. | Work at home. |
| 으로 / 로 | toward; by means of; into | 버스로 가요. / 한국어로 말해요. | Go by bus. / Speak in Korean. |
| 부터 | from (starting point) | 월요일부터 | from Monday |
| 까지 | until / to (endpoint) | 금요일까지 | until Friday |
| 한테 / 에게 | to (person, informal/formal) | 친구한테 줘요. | Give to a friend. |
| 한테서 / 에게서 | from (person) | 선생님에게서 배워요. | Learn from the teacher. |

**에 vs 에서**
- 학교에 **있어요**. — I am **at** school. (state → 에)  
- 학교에서 **공부해요**. — I study **at** school. (action → 에서)

### 4.5 Possessive & Connective

| Particle | Meaning | Example | English |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| 의 | of / 's | 친구의 책 | friend's book |
| 와 / 과 | and / with (written/formal) | 빵과 우유 | bread and milk |
| 하고 | and / with (spoken) | 친구하고 가요. | Go with a friend. |
| 이랑 / 랑 | and / with (casual) | 형이랑 | with older brother |
| (이)나 | or / about | 커피나 차 | coffee or tea |

**Note:** 의 is often pronounced like *e* in speech: 친구의 → *chingu-e*.

### 4.6 Also, Only, From Among, etc.

| Particle | Meaning | Example | English |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| 도 | also / even | 저도 가요. | I go too. |
| 만 | only | 물만 마셔요. | (I) only drink water. |
| 부터 | starting from | 처음부터 | from the beginning |
| 마다 | every | 날마다 | every day |
| 쯤 | about / around | 3시쯤 | around 3 o'clock |
| 처럼 / 같이 | like / as | 천사처럼 | like an angel |
| 보다 | than (comparison) | 커피보다 차를 더 좋아해요. | I like tea more than coffee. |

### 4.7 Question / Quotation

| Particle | Meaning | Example | English |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| 은/는가요? etc. | (see endings) | — | — |
| (이)라고 | quote "N" | 학생이라고 해요. | (They) say (he/she is) a student. |
| 고 | quote clause | 간다고 했어요. | (They) said (someone) is going. |

## 5. Verbs & Adjectives (용언)

In Korean, **verbs (동사)** and **descriptive verbs/adjectives (형용사)** conjugate similarly. Dictionary form ends in **~다**.

| Type | Dictionary | Meaning |
|------|------------|---------|
| Action verb | 가다 | to go |
| Action verb | 먹다 | to eat |
| Descriptive | 크다 | to be big |
| Descriptive | 예쁘다 | to be pretty |

**Important:** Korean "adjectives" act like verbs — they do **not** need "to be" in the predicate.

- English: She **is** pretty.  
- Korean: 그녀는 예뻐요. (No separate "is")

### 5.1 Verb Stems

Remove **다** to get the stem:

| Dictionary | Stem | Meaning |
|------------|------|---------|
| 가다 | 가- | go |
| 먹다 | 먹- | eat |
| 쓰다 | 쓰- | write / use / bitter |
| 듣다 | 듣- | listen |

### 5.2 Polite Informal Ending: ~아요 / ~어요 / ~여요 (해요체)

Most useful everyday polite style.

**Rule:**
1. Stem vowel is **ㅏ or ㅗ** → **~아요**  
2. Other vowels → **~어요**  
3. Stem ends in **하** → **해요** (하다 → 해요)

| Dictionary | Polite | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 가다 | 가요 | go / goes |
| 오다 | 와요 | come |
| 보다 | 봐요 | see / watch |
| 먹다 | 먹어요 | eat |
| 읽다 | 읽어요 | read |
| 쓰다 | 써요 | write |
| 마시다 | 마셔요 | drink |
| 하다 | 해요 | do |
| 공부하다 | 공부해요 | study |
| 크다 | 커요 | is big |
| 좋다 | 좋아요 | is good / like (as 좋아하다 for "like") |

**Examples**
- 지금 집에 가요. — I'm going home now.  
- 한국어를 공부해요. — I study Korean.  
- 날씨가 좋아요. — The weather is good.  
- 커피를 마셔요. — I drink coffee.

### 5.3 Formal Polite: ~습니다 / ~ㅂ니다 (합쇼체)

| Stem ends with | Ending | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| Consonant | ~습니다 | 먹습니다 (eat) |
| Vowel | ~ㅂ니다 | 갑니다 (go) |

- 만나서 반갑습니다. — Nice to meet you.  
- 감사합니다. — Thank you.  
- 질문 있습니다. — I have a question.

### 5.4 Casual / Intimate: ~아 / ~어 (해체)

Same vowel harmony as ~아요/~어요, drop 요.

- 어디 가? — Where are you going?  
- 밥 먹어. — Eat (your meal).  
- 좋아. — (It's) good. / I like it.

### 5.5 Plain / Dictionary Style: ~ㄴ다 / ~는다 / ~다

Used in writing, diaries, narration, some inner monologue.

| Type | Ending | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| Action, consonant stem | ~는다 | 먹는다 |
| Action, vowel stem | ~ㄴ다 | 간다 |
| Descriptive | ~다 | 크다 / 좋다 |

## 6. Irregular Verbs (불규칙)

Memorize the common patterns; they appear constantly.

### 6.1 ㄷ Irregular

ㄷ → ㄹ before a vowel ending.

| Dictionary | +아/어 | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 듣다 | 들어요 | listen / hear |
| 걷다 | 걸어요 | walk |
| 묻다 | 물어요 | ask |

- 음악을 들어요. — I listen to music.  
- 매일 걸어요. — I walk every day.

### 6.2 ㅂ Irregular

ㅂ → 우 (often) before vowel; then 우 + 어 → 워.

| Dictionary | Polite | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 춥다 | 추워요 | cold (weather) |
| 덥다 | 더워요 | hot (weather) |
| 어렵다 | 어려워요 | difficult |
| 쉽다 | 쉬워요 | easy |
| 돕다 | 도와요 | help (special: 오) |
| 곱다 | 고와요 | pretty/lovely (old-fashioned/literary) |

- 오늘 정말 추워요. — It's really cold today.  
- 한국어가 어려워요. — Korean is difficult.

### 6.3 ㅅ Irregular

ㅅ drops before vowel endings.

| Dictionary | Polite | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 낫다 | 나아요 | get better / recover |
| 짓다 | 지어요 | build / make (rice) |
| 잇다 | 이어요 | connect |

### 6.4 ㅎ Irregular (mostly adjectives)

ㅎ drops; vowels often contract.

| Dictionary | Polite | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 그렇다 | 그래요 | be so / like that |
| 빨갛다 | 빨개요 | be red |
| 노랗다 | 노래요 | be yellow |
| 이렇다 | 이래요 | be like this |

### 6.5 르 Irregular

르 → ㄹㄹ and 라/러.

| Dictionary | Polite | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 모르다 | 몰라요 | not know |
| 다르다 | 달라요 | be different |
| 빠르다 | 빨라요 | be fast |
| 부르다 | 불러요 | call / sing |

- 잘 몰라요. — I don't really know.  
- 너무 빨라요. — It's too fast.

### 6.6 으 Drop

If stem ends in 으, 으 drops before 아/어.

| Dictionary | Polite | English |
|------------|--------|---------|
| 쓰다 | 써요 | write / use |
| 크다 | 커요 | be big |
| 바쁘다 | 바빠요 | be busy |
| 예쁘다 | 예뻐요 | be pretty |

## 7. Tenses & Aspects

### 7.1 Present

Covered by ~아요/어요, ~습니다, etc.

- 지금 일해요. — I work / am working now.

### 7.2 Past: ~았/었/였-

Same vowel harmony as present, then + 어요 / 습니다.

| Dictionary | Past polite | English |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| 가다 | 갔어요 | went |
| 먹다 | 먹었어요 | ate |
| 하다 | 했어요 | did |
| 보다 | 봤어요 | saw / watched |
| 듣다 | 들었어요 | listened / heard |
| 춥다 | 추웠어요 | was cold |

- 어제 영화를 봤어요. — I watched a movie yesterday.  
- 아침에 커피를 마셨어요. — I drank coffee in the morning.

### 7.3 Future / Supposition: ~(으)ㄹ 거예요

| Stem | Form | Example | English |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| Vowel / no batchim | ~ㄹ 거예요 | 갈 거예요 | will go |
| Consonant | ~을 거예요 | 먹을 거예요 | will eat |
| ㄹ batchim | ~ㄹ 거예요 | 만들 거예요 | will make |

- 내일 친구를 만날 거예요. — I will meet a friend tomorrow.  
- 주말에 쉴 거예요. — I will rest on the weekend.

### 7.4 Progressive: ~고 있다

- 지금 공부하고 있어요. — I am studying now.  
- 비가 오고 있어요. — It is raining.

### 7.5 Want to: ~고 싶다

- 한국에 가고 싶어요. — I want to go to Korea.  
- 뭘 먹고 싶어요? — What do you want to eat?

**Past want:** 가고 싶었어요 — I wanted to go.

### 7.6 Have you ever / experience: ~아/어 보다

- 김치 먹어 봤어요? — Have you tried kimchi?  
- 스키 타 보고 싶어요. — I want to try skiing.

### 7.7 Intention: ~(으)려고 하다

- 한국어를 배우려고 해요. — I intend to learn Korean.  
- 집에 일찍 가려고요. — I'm planning to go home early.

### 7.8 Ability / Possibility: ~(으)ㄹ 수 있다 / 없다

- 한글을 읽을 수 있어요. — I can read Hangul.  
- 내일은 갈 수 없어요. — I can't go tomorrow.

### 7.9 Obligation: ~아/어야 하다 / 되다

- 약을 먹어야 해요. — I have to take medicine.  
- 지금 가야 돼요. — I have to go now. (spoken 돼요 common)

### 7.10 Permission: ~아/어도 되다

- 들어와도 돼요? — May I come in?  
- 사진을 찍어도 돼요. — You may take photos.

### 7.11 Prohibition: ~(으)면 안 되다

- 여기서 담배를 피우면 안 돼요. — You must not smoke here.

## 8. Speech Levels & Honorifics

### 8.1 Common Levels (simplified)

| Level | Ending example | When to use |
|-------|----------------|-------------|
| Formal polite | ~습니다 | Presentations, strangers, service, formal writing |
| Informal polite | ~아요/어요 | Default daily polite (most learners start here) |
| Casual | ~아/어 | Close friends, younger people, family (careful) |
| Formal written | ~ㄴ다 | Novels, news, diaries |

### 8.2 Honorific Subject Marker & Verb

Show respect to the **subject** (often older people, customers, teachers).

| Plain | Honorific | English |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 가다 | 가시다 | to go |
| 먹다 | 드시다 / 잡수시다 | to eat |
| 마시다 | 드시다 | to drink |
| 있다 | 계시다 | to be / stay (people) |
| 자다 | 주무시다 | to sleep |
| 말하다 | 말씀하시다 | to speak |
| 주다 (to elder) | 드리다 | to give (humble) |

**Honorific ending:** ~시-

- 선생님이 오셨어요. — The teacher came.  
- 할머니께서 주무세요. — Grandmother is sleeping.  
- 말씀하세요. — Please speak. / Go ahead.

**Humble "I give / do for someone respected":**
- 선물을 드렸어요. — I gave a gift (to someone I respect).

### 8.3 Address Terms (quick)

| Korean | Use |
|--------|-----|
| 씨 | After full/given name: polite neutral (민수 씨) |
| 님 | Higher respect (선생님, 고객님) |
| 아/야 | Casual vocative (민수야 / 지나) |
| 선배 / 후배 | Senior / junior (school, work culture) |
| 형 / 누나 / 오빠 / 언니 | Older brother/sister terms (depends on speaker gender) |

## 9. Essential Grammar Patterns

### 9.1 Noun Sentences: 이에요 / 예요 / 입니다

| After | Form | Example | English |
|-------|------|---------|---------|
| Consonant | 이에요 | 학생이에요 | is a student |
| Vowel | 예요 | 의사예요 | is a doctor |
| Formal | 입니다 | 학생입니다 | is a student |

**Negative:** 이/가 아니에요  
- 학생이 아니에요. — (I) am not a student.

### 9.2 Existence: 있다 / 없다

- 시간이 있어요. — I have time. / There is time.  
- 돈이 없어요. — I don't have money.  
-  comm 옆에 은행이 있어요. — There is a bank next to the station.

**Location question:** 어디에 있어요? — Where is it?

### 9.3 This / That

| Korean | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 이 | this (near me) |
| 그 | that (near you / known) |
| 저 | that (over there) |
| 이것 / 이거 | this thing |
| 그것 / 그거 | that thing |
| 저것 / 저거 | that thing over there |
| 여기 | here |
| 거기 | there |

## 22. Sample Dialogues

### 22.1 Café

**직원:** 안녕하세요. 주문하시겠어요?  
*(Hello. Would you like to order?)*

**손님:** 아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.  
*(One iced americano, please.)*

**직원:** 드시고 가세요, 포장이세요?  
*(For here or to go?)*

**손님:** 포장해 주세요. 얼마예요?  
*(To go, please. How much is it?)*

**직원:** 4,500원입니다.  
*(It's 4,500 won.)*

**손님:** 카드로 할게요. 감사합니다.  
*(I'll pay by card. Thank you.)*

### 22.2 Meeting Someone

**A:** 안녕하세요. 처음 뵙겠습니다. 제 이름은 지민이에요.  
*(Hello. Nice to meet you for the first time. My name is Jimin.)*

**B:** 안녕하세요. 저는 마크예요. 어디서 오셨어요?  
*(Hello. I'm Mark. Where are you from?)*

**A:** 서울에서 왔어요. 마크 씨는요?  
*(I'm from Seoul. How about you, Mark?)*

**B:** 캐나다에서 왔어요. 한국어를 공부하고 있어요.  
*(I'm from Canada. I'm studying Korean.)*

**A:** 와, 한국어 잘하시네요!  
*(Wow, your Korean is good!)*

**B:** 아직 잘 못해요. 천천히 말해 주세요.  
*(I still can't speak well. Please speak slowly.)*

### 22.3 Making Plans

**A:** 이번 주말에 뭐 할 거예요?  
*(What will you do this weekend?)*

**B:** 아직 모르겠어요. 같이 영화 볼까요?  
*(I'm not sure yet. Shall we watch a movie together?)*

**A:** 좋아요. 언제가 좋아요?  
*(Sounds good. When is good for you?)*

**B:** 토요일 오후 어때요?  
*(How about Saturday afternoon?)*

**A:** 괜찮아요. 어디서 만날까요?  
*(That's fine. Where shall we meet?)*

**B:** 강남역에서 만나요.  
*(Let's meet at Gangnam Station.)*

## 23. Study Roadmap (What "Proper" Practice Looks Like)

### Beginner (0–3 months)
1. Master Hangul reading/writing in a few days; review batchim daily.  
2. Memorize particles: 은/는, 이/가, 을/를, 에, 에서, 도, 만.  
3. Conjugate ~아요/어요 for 50 core verbs.  
4. Learn numbers + 개, 명, 시, 분.  
5. Self-intro + café/restaurant phrases.  

### Elementary (3–9 months)
1. Past, future, ~고 싶다, ~수 있다, ~아야 하다.  
2. Connectors: ~고, ~아서, ~지만, ~면, ~는데.  
3. Honorific ~시- and basic humble forms.  
4. 500–1000 high-frequency words.  
5. Short diary entries (3–5 sentences/day).  

### Intermediate goals
1. Relative clauses modifying nouns.  
2. Reported speech, indirect questions.  
3. Passive/causative awareness (되다/아지다, ~이/히/리/기, ~게 하다).  
4. Native content with subtitles → without.  
5. TOPIK II grammar lists if you want certification structure.

### Daily routine (45–60 min)
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 10 min | Review Anki/flashcards (vocab + example sentences) |
| 10 min | Grammar point + 5 self-made sentences |
| 10 min | Listening (podcast / drama scene) shadowing |
| 10 min | Reading graded text or webtoon with dictionary |
| 10 min | Speaking aloud or language exchange |

## 24. Extra Grammar You Will Meet Soon

### 24.1 Passive-ish / Become: ~아/어지다
- 날씨가 추워졌어요. — The weather became cold.  
- 문이 열렸어요. — The door was opened / opened.

### 24.2 Try & See: ~아/어 보다 (already covered) + ~다 보면
- 하다 보면 늘어요. — If you keep doing it, you'll improve.

### 24.3 Seems / Looks: ~ㄴ/은/는 것 같다
- 비가 올 것 같아요. — It seems like it will rain.  
- 맛있을 것 같아요. — It looks like it would be delicious.

### 24.4 I heard: ~다고 하다 / ~대요
- 내일 쉰대요. — I heard they're off tomorrow.

### 24.5 While / As soon as
- ~으면서 — while doing (same subject)  
- ~자마자 — as soon as  
  - 집에 오자마자 씻었어요. — I washed as soon as I came home.

### 24.6 Even if: ~아/어도
- 바빠도 운동해요. — Even if I'm busy, I exercise.

### 24.7 Only / Nothing but: ~밖에 + negative
- 물밖에 안 마셨어요. — I drank nothing but water.

### 24.8 Almost did: ~을/ㄹ 뻔하다
- 넘어질 뻔했어요. — I almost fell.

### 24.9 Planning: ~을/ㄹ 예정이다 / 계획이다
- 내년에 한국에 갈 예정이에요. — I plan to go to Korea next year.

### 24.10 Nominalization: ~기 / ~는 것
- 한국어 배우기가 재미있어요. — Learning Korean is fun.  
- 운동하는 것이 중요해요. — Exercising is important.

## 25. Cultural Notes for Learners (brief)

1. **Age and hierarchy matter** in language (형/누나/오빠/언니, honorifics). When unsure, use polite ~요 form.  
2. **우리** often means "my" for in-groups: 우리 집, 우리 엄마, 우리 회사.  
3. **Names + 씨** is safe among adults who are not close; don't use 씨 with your own name.  
4. **거절 (refusing)** is often soft: 조금 어려울 것 같아요 (It might be a bit difficult).  
5. **Eating together** culture: 잘 먹겠습니다 (before meal), 잘 먹었습니다 (after).  
6. **Two number systems** — mix-ups are normal; practice with money and time daily.

## Closing Note

Korean rewards **pattern recognition** more than word-by-word translation. Every time you learn a verb, practice:

1. Present polite  
2. Past  
3. Future  
4. Negative  
5. One sentence about *your* life  

Example with **배우다** (to learn):  
- 지금 한국어를 배워요. — I'm learning Korean now.  
- 작년부터 배웠어요. — I've been learning since last year.  
- 더 배울 거예요. — I will learn more.  
- 아직 한자를 안 배워요. — I don't learn Hanja yet.  
- 노래로 한국어를 배우고 싶어요. — I want to learn Korean through songs.
