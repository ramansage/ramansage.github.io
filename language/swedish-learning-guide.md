# Swedish (Svenska) Learning Guide
### Beginner / Introductory Resource

**Goal:** Build a solid foundation in Swedish pronunciation, core grammar, high-frequency vocabulary, and everyday patterns so you can form real sentences and keep expanding.

**How to use this guide**
1. Learn pronunciation and the alphabet quirks (å, ä, ö) first.
2. Master word order (V2) and the en/ett gender system early — they run through everything.
3. Learn present, past, and perfect of common verbs.
4. Drill vocabulary lists in small daily sets (10–15 items) with one sentence each.
5. Speak examples out loud; Swedish spelling and sound diverge often.

**Notation**
| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **svenska** | Swedish |
| *IPA / hint* | Pronunciation help (approximate) |
| (English) | Translation |
| en / ett | Common / neuter gender |
| ~ | Pattern slot |

> Swedish has **two grammatical genders** for nouns: **en-words** (common) and **ett-words** (neuter). Always learn the article with the noun.

# Part I — Sounds & Writing

## 1. Alphabet & Special Letters

Swedish uses the Latin alphabet plus three extra letters at the end:

| Letter | Approx. sound | Example | English |
|--------|---------------|---------|---------|
| **Å å** | like *o* in "or" / *aw* | åka | go / ride |
| **Ä ä** | like *e* in "bed" (open) | äta | eat |
| **Ö ö** | like *i* in "bird" (British) / French *eu* | öl | beer |

**Alphabet order note:** … Z, **Å, Ä, Ö** (not mixed into A/O).

### 1.1 Vowels (long vs short)

Vowel **length** matters. A long vowel is usually followed by **one** consonant; a short vowel by **two** (or a consonant cluster).

| Letter | Long (approx.) | Short (approx.) | Pair example |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| a | *ah* (father) | shorter *a* | tak (roof) / tack (thanks) |
| e | *ey* / *eh* | short *e* | se (see) / sett |
| i | *ee* | short *i* | sil / sill |
| o | *oo* or *oh* (varies) | short *o* | bot / bott |
| u | front *u* (rounded) | short | ful / full |
| y | French *u* / German *ü* | short | byt / bytt |
| å | *aw* | shorter | mål / mått |
| ä | open *e* | shorter | rät / rätt |
| ö | *ø* | shorter | röt / rött |

### 1.2 Consonants worth learning early

| Spelling | Sound tip | Example | English |
|----------|-----------|---------|---------|
| **kj / tj / k** before soft vowel | *sh*-like / *ch* | köpa, tjej, kyrka | buy, girl, church |
| **sk** before e,i,y,ä,ö | *sh*-like (sj-sound) | skina, sked | shine, spoon |
| **sk** before a,o,u,å | *sk* | ska, skola | will/shall, school |
| **sj / stj / skj** | sj-sound (*sh* / *hw*-ish; varies by dialect) | sju, stjärna | seven, star |
| **rs** | often *sh*-like | fors | rapid |
| **g** before e,i,y,ä,ö | often *y* as in "yes" | göra, gift | do, married |
| **g** otherwise | hard *g* | gå, god | go, good |
| **gn** | *ngn* | vagn | wagon |
| **ng** | *ng* (no hard g) | många | many |
| **rd, rl, rn, rt** | retroflex in many accents | bord, barn | table, child |

### 1.3 Melody (prosody)

Swedish is a **pitch-accent** language (two word accents in Central Swedish). Beginners: focus first on **clear long/short vowels** and stress on the **first syllable** of most native words; loanwords vary.

- **Stress:** *KOCKa* (cook), *TAlar* (speaks)
- Polite tip: listen and shadow native audio early (SVT, podcasts, Musik).

# Part II — Core Grammar

## 2. Word Order: The V2 Rule

In main clauses, the **finite verb is in second position** (not always second word — second *slot*).

| English | Swedish | Structure |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| I speak Swedish. | Jag talar svenska. | Subject – Verb – Object |
| Today I speak Swedish. | Idag talar jag svenska. | Adverb – **Verb** – Subject – Object |
| I don't speak French. | Jag talar inte franska. | Subject – Verb – **inte** – Object |

**Yes/no questions:** Verb first.

- Talar du svenska? — Do you speak Swedish?
- Bor hon här? — Does she live here?

**Question words:** Question word first, then verb.

- Vad heter du? — What are you called? / What's your name?
- Var bor du? — Where do you live?
- När kommer han? — When is he coming?
- Varför lär du dig svenska? — Why are you learning Swedish?
- Hur mår du? — How are you? (How do you feel?)
- Vem är det? — Who is that?
- Vilken bok läser du? — Which book are you reading?

### 2.1 Subordinate clauses (beginner preview)

In subordinate clauses, **inte** and many adverbs come **before** the verb (no V2).

| Main | Subordinate |
|------|-------------|
| Jag **kommer inte** idag. | … att jag **inte kommer** idag. |
| I am not coming today. | … that I am not coming today. |

Common subordinators: **att** (that), **om** (if/whether), **eftersom** (because), **när** (when), **som** (who/which/that).

- Jag vet att hon **inte bor** här. — I know that she doesn't live here.

## 3. Nouns: en / ett, Plural, Definite Form

### 3.1 Indefinite articles

| Gender | Article | Example | English |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| Common | **en** | en bok | a book |
| Neuter | **ett** | ett hus | a house |

There is **no** grammatical “the” as a separate word in basic definite form — Swedish usually **suffixes** the article.

### 3.2 Definite forms (the)

| Indefinite | Definite | English |
|------------|----------|---------|
| en bok | boken | the book |
| en bil | bilen | the car |
| en flicka | flickan | the girl |
| ett hus | huset | the house |
| ett äpple | äpplet | the apple |
| ett barn | barnet | the child |

**Rules of thumb**
- en-words → add **-en** or **-n**
- ett-words → add **-et** or **-t**

### 3.3 Plurals (main patterns)

Swedish plurals fall into groups. Learn plural with every noun.

| Group | Pattern | Indef. sg | Indef. pl | Def. pl | English |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1 | -or | en flicka | flickor | flickorna | girl(s) |
| 2 | -ar | en bil | bilar | bilarna | car(s) |
| 3 | -er | en park | parker | parkerna | park(s) |
| 4 | -n | ett äpple | äpplen | äpplena | apple(s) |
| 5 | no change | ett hus | hus | husen | house(s) |
| irregular | various | en man | män | männen | man/men |
| | | en bok | böcker | böckerna | book(s) |
| | | ett barn | barn | barnen | child/children |

**Examples**
- Boken ligger på bordet. — The book is lying on the table.
- Jag har två bilar. — I have two cars.
- Barnen leker i parken. — The children are playing in the park.

### 3.4 Double definite (adjective + noun)

When an adjective modifies a definite noun, Swedish often uses **den/det/de** + adjective **-a** + definite noun:

| Form | Example | English |
|------|---------|---------|
| den + -a + en-word | den stora bilen | the big car |
| det + -a + ett-word | det stora huset | the big house |
| de + -a + plural | de stora bilarna | the big cars |

- Jag vill ha den röda boken. — I want the red book.

## 4. Personal Pronouns

| Subject | Object | Possessive (en) | Possessive (ett) | Possessive (pl) | English |
|---------|--------|-----------------|------------------|-----------------|---------|
| jag | mig | min | mitt | mina | I / me / my |
| du | dig | din | ditt | dina | you (sing.) |
| han | honom | hans | hans | hans | he / him / his |
| hon | henne | hennes | hennes | hennes | she / her |
| hen | hen / henom* | hens | hens | hens | gender-neutral they |
| den | den | dess | dess | dess | it (en-word) |
| det | det | dess | dess | dess | it (ett-word) / dummy “it” |
| vi | oss | vår | vårt | våra | we / us / our |
| ni | er | er | ert | era | you (pl./formal) |
| de | dem | deras | deras | deras | they / them / their |

\* *hen* object form varies in use (*hen* / *henom*).

**Important:** **det** is also a dummy subject:

- Det regnar. — It is raining.
- Det är kallt idag. — It is cold today.
- Det finns en bok här. — There is a book here.

**Examples**
- Jag ser dig. — I see you.
- Det är min bok. — It is my book.
- Det är mitt hus. — It is my house.
- Det är mina böcker. — They are my books.
- Hon hjälper oss. — She helps us.

### 4.1 Subject–object tip: de / dem

In speech, both often sound like “dom.” In writing:

- **de** = subject (they)
- **dem** = object (them)

- De ser dem. — They see them.

|--------|---------------------------|
| jag | talar |
| du | talar |
| hon/han | talar |
| vi | talar |
| ni | talar |
| de | talar |

### 5.1 Infinitive & present

| Form | Pattern | Example | English |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Infinitive | often -a | att tala | to speak |
| Present | -r | talar | speak(s) |

### 5.4 Past vs perfect

| Tense | Form | Example | English |
|-------|------|---------|---------|
| Past (preteritum) | simple past | Jag **köpte** en bok igår. | I bought a book yesterday. |
| Perfect | har + supine | Jag **har köpt** en bok. | I have bought a book. |
| Pluperfect | hade + supine | Jag **hade köpt** den. | I had bought it. |

**Rule of thumb**
- Specific finished past time (igår, 2010…) → often **past**
- Experience / relevance now → **perfect**

- Har du varit i Sverige? — Have you been to Sweden?
- Jag var i Sverige förra året. — I was in Sweden last year.

### 5.5 Future

Swedish often uses **present** with future time, or **ska** / **kommer att**.

| Form | Example | English |
|------|---------|---------|
| present + time | Jag reser imorgon. | I leave/travel tomorrow. |
| ska | Jag ska studera. | I will / am going to study. (plan/intention) |
| kommer att | Det kommer att regna. | It will rain. (prediction) |
| tänker | Jag tänker stanna. | I intend to stay. |

### 5.6 Modal verbs

| Modal | Meaning | Example | English |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| kan | can / know how | Jag kan simma. | I can swim. |
| ska | will / shall / be going to | Vi ska gå nu. | We will/shall go now. |
| vill | want to | Jag vill sova. | I want to sleep. |
| måste | must / have to | Jag måste arbeta. | I must work. |
| bör | should | Du bör vila. | You should rest. |
| får | may / get to / be allowed | Får jag komma in? | May I come in? |
| behöver | need to | Du behöver inte oroa dig. | You don’t need to worry. |
| skulle | would / should (past of ska) | Jag skulle vilja ha kaffe. | I would like coffee. |

Modals take the **infinitive without att**:

- Jag kan **tala** svenska. (not *att tala*)

### 5.7 Negation

**inte** = not (usually after the finite verb in main clauses).

- Jag talar **inte** tyska. — I don’t speak German.
- Hon är **inte** hemma. — She is not home.
- Kommer du **inte**? — Aren’t you coming?

Other negatives:

| Swedish | English |
|---------|---------|
| aldrig | never |
| ingenting / inget | nothing |
| ingen / inget / inga | no one / no (determiner) |
| ingenstans | nowhere |
| inte ens | not even |
| knappt | hardly |

- Jag har aldrig varit där. — I have never been there.
- Jag har ingen bil. — I have no car.
- Det finns inget bröd. — There is no bread.

- Exceptions are common — learn place + preposition as a chunk.

**Time chunks**
- på morgonen — in the morning  
- på dagen — in the daytime  
- på kvällen — in the evening  
- på natten — at night  
- i januari — in January

## 18. Sample Dialogues

### 18.1 Café

**Barista:** Hej! Vad får det lov att vara?  
*(Hi! What can I get you?)*

**Kund:** Hej! En kaffe, tack.  
*(Hi! A coffee, please.)*

**Barista:** Ska det vara något mer?  
*(Anything else?)*

**Kund:** En kanelbulle också, tack. Vad kostar det?  
*(A cinnamon bun too, please. How much is it?)*

**Barista:** Det blir 65 kronor.  
*(That’ll be 65 kronor.)*

**Kund:** Kan jag betala med kort?  
*(Can I pay by card?)*

**Barista:** Absolut. Varsågod!  
*(Absolutely. Here you go!)*

### 18.2 Meeting someone

**A:** Hej! Jag heter Noah. Trevligt att träffas.  
*(Hi! I’m Noah. Nice to meet you.)*

**B:** Hej Noah! Jag heter Maja. Varifrån kommer du?  
*(Hi Noah! I’m Maja. Where are you from?)*

**A:** Jag kommer från Kanada, men jag bor i Stockholm nu.  
*(I’m from Canada, but I live in Stockholm now.)*

**B:** Kul! Vad gör du här?  
*(Cool! What are you doing here?)*

**A:** Jag studerar svenska och arbetar extra på ett café.  
*(I’m studying Swedish and working part-time at a café.)*

### 18.3 Asking for directions

**A:** Ursäkta, var ligger närmaste tunnelbanestation?  
*(Excuse me, where is the nearest metro station?)*

**B:** Gå rakt fram, och ta sedan till vänster. Den ligger på höger sida.  
*(Go straight ahead, then turn left. It’s on the right-hand side.)*

**A:** Tack så mycket!  
*(Thank you very much!)*

**B:** Ingen orsak!  
*(You’re welcome!)*

2. ___ hus

4. ___ bil  
5. ___ barn  

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

**B. Put the verb second**  
1. (idag / jag / arbetar) → ________  
2. (svenska / talar / hon) → ________  

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

## 23. “Start Today” Checklist

- [ ] Learn å, ä, ö sounds and write 10 words with them  
- [ ] Memorize: jag, du, han, hon, vi, ni, de + är/har  
- [ ] Learn 15 en-words and 10 ett-words with articles  
- [ ] Conjugate in present: tala, gå, göra, komma, se  
- [ ] Practice V2: *Idag lär jag mig svenska.*  
- [ ] Memorize: Hej, Tack, Ursäkta, Förlåt, Varsågod, Ja/Nej/Jo  
- [ ] Order something in Swedish (even to yourself): *En kaffe, tack*  
- [ ] Listen to 5 minutes of easy Swedish

## 24. Closing Note

Swedish is comparatively **regular in verbs** (no person endings) but demands attention to:

1. **en / ett**  
2. **V2 word order**  
3. **Definite suffixes**  
4. **Adjective agreement**  
5. **Supine vs past participle** (as you go further)

Every new verb, practice:

1. Present  
2. Past  
3. Perfect (*har + supine*)  
4. Negative  
5. One sentence about your life  

**Example with lära sig (learn):**  
- Jag lär mig svenska. — I am learning Swedish.  
- Jag lärde mig mycket igår. — I learned a lot yesterday.  
- Jag har lärt mig alfabetet. — I have learned the alphabet.  
- Jag lär mig inte tyska nu. — I’m not learning German now.  
- Jag vill lära mig mer. — I want to learn more.

Lycka till! (Good luck!)
